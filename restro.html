Overview

Restro is a restaurant ordering and delivery app. Along with regular food orders, it offers monthly meal subscription plans, so regular meals arrive on time without ordering every day.

The problem

  • People miss breakfast or lunch because of busy schedules. Ordering and delivery take time, so sometimes they skip the meal completely.
  • Restaurants needed an easy way to offer monthly subscriptions to their customers.

The goal

  • Make daily food ordering simple, and get meals to users on time.
  • Design a way for users to manage their subscriptions and start one easily.

My role

I was the lead UX designer, UX researcher and visual designer, in charge of the app from idea to delivery.

My work covered interviews, paper and digital wireframes, low- and high-fidelity prototypes, usability research, accessibility, design iterations, information architecture and responsive design.

Understanding the user

User research, personas, problem statements, user journey maps

What the research showed

At first we thought the main problem was delivery and its timing. After research, we found consumers did have some delivery issues worth fixing.

But interviews and surveys showed a bigger issue: people often missed breakfast or lunch because of their busy schedules. A subscription would help them, and also students and anyone else who wants regular daily meals.

Pain points

  1. No time to order. Many users are so busy they skip meals. A monthly plan, where you choose what to eat and on which days, would remove that stress.
  2. Choosing what to eat. Users have little time to decide, so smart recommendations based on what they ate and liked before are useful.
  3. Delivery people can't find the address. Delivery partners often struggle to find the location. Letting users add directions for the delivery person would help.

Findings

We ran two unmoderated usability studies: one after the low-fidelity prototype and one after the high-fidelity prototype. Both used almost the same tasks, so we could compare the flow, time taken and problems found.

Round 1

  • More flexible subscription plans
  • More flexibility in choosing meals
  • Clearer cues for what to do next

Round 2

  • Better sorting of subscription plans
  • More flexible choice of date and time
  • A better colour palette

Refining the design

Mockups, high-fidelity prototype, accessibility

Mockups

I wanted an easy-going design and colour palette with clearer cues. At first I used only red and a lighter, almost pink shade of it, but it felt like too much red. We wanted something simple and elegant.

So I combined the same red with violet to create a gradient. Otherwise, I kept things as simple as possible. After watching how users interacted with the design, I adjusted the elements, spacing and placement of components.

High-fidelity prototype

The final prototype has a cleaner user flow and a cleaner design.

Accessibility

  • Colour contrast: text has a contrast ratio of at least 4.5:1 against its background, and icons and UI components at least 3:1 against nearby colours. The primary red (#B60021) on white has a contrast ratio of 6.97:1. It passes WCAG AA for normal text, and WCAG AA and AAA for large text.
  • Text size: no text is smaller than 10 points.
  • Layout: clean and simple, and consistent across every screen.
  • Navigation and components: simple navigation with clear cues for what to do next, and icons to help. Components with the same job look the same, thanks to a design system made for this app.
4

Going forward

Takeaways, next steps

Impact

Users gave the design positive reviews. Two things they said:

  • "I want to try a monthly subscription plan to organise my food schedule."
  • "It seems it can solve my breakfast problem."

What I learned

The research, interviews and surveys made me see things from the user's side and let go of my own assumptions. The accessibility research made me more empathetic. I learned that we should think about every person who might use the product, and when something isn't possible yet, look for another solution.

Next steps

  1. Let users choose how many meals they want each day: breakfast and lunch, or all three meals.
  2. Add dine-in and pick-up options for subscription meals.
  3. Let users cancel or delay a meal, or change the delivery time, when they're busy or away.
  4. Run another round of usability testing to check the pain points are fixed.
  5. Do more user research to find new needs.
